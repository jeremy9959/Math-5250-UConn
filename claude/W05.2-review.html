    Mathematical content

    1. Lines 32–36. The coefficients a_i depend on j. Recommend writing a_{ij} (or a_i^{(j)}).
    2. Line 48. The computation checks the identity only for basis vectors v_k, w_j. Recommend one sentence saying that both sides are linear in v and conjugate-linear in w, so the identity extends to all v, w. Uniqueness follows because the values f^*(w_j) are forced.
    3. Line 50. “prove the linearity of the adjoint without appealing to bases”: Roman’s basis-free argument establishes the existence of the adjoint (via the Riesz representation theorem). Recommend “prove the existence of the adjoint”.
    4. Lines 52–56. f: V\to W here, so [f]_E needs a basis of W as well. Recommend either assuming V=W or writing [f^*]_{F,E}=\overline{[f]_{E,F}}^{\top} for orthonormal bases E of V and F of W.
    5. Lines 60–68. The last equality, [u]^{\top}\overline{\overline{[f]}^{\top}[v]} = \langle u, f^*(v)\rangle, presupposes [f^*(v)]_E=\overline{[f]_E}^{\top}[v]_E, which is the claim. The computation actually shows that the map with matrix \overline{[f]_E}^{\top} satisfies the defining identity; recommend concluding “so by uniqueness of the adjoint, [f^*]_E=\overline{[f]_E}^{\top}”.
    6. Lines 97–99 (item 3). The argument uses \ker(f)=\image(f^*)^{\perp}, which is item 1 applied to f^* together with f^{**}=f. Recommend citing that. Only the forward direction is proved; recommend adding “Conversely, if f^* is surjective then \ker(f)=V^{\perp}=0.”
    7. Line 115. “Equivalently, f is diagonalizable if …” should read “unitarily diagonalizable”.
    8. Lines 132 and 180. Both lines refer to the structure of real normal operators, but cite different results (Theorem 10.19, p. 247 and Theorem 10.14, p. 216). Recommend checking both against the linked edition. Page 216 for Theorem 10.14 lies before page 247 for 10.19, which is consistent, but the two sentences describe the same result.
    9. Line 138. “the inverse of a normal operator is normal” — add “invertible”.
    10. Line 147. The display \langle f(v),f(v)\rangle=\langle v,f^*f(v)\rangle=\langle f^*(v),f^*(v)\rangle omits the step that uses normality. Recommend \langle v,f^*f(v)\rangle=\langle v,ff^*(v)\rangle=\langle f^*(v),f^*(v)\rangle.
    11. Line 155. g^k=(f^*)^kf^k uses that f and f^* commute. Recommend saying so.
    12. Line 158 and Remark at 175. The argument shows that linear factors (x-\lambda) of the minimal polynomial occur with multiplicity one. The Remark asserts square-freeness over \R, including irreducible quadratic factors. The same argument applied to the normal operator q(f), for q an irreducible real quadratic, gives this; recommend noting it.
    13. Lines 196–201. With the usual convention that column j of [f]_E is [f(u_j)]_E, the entry is [f]_{ij}=\langle f(u_j),u_i\rangle, not \langle f(u_i),u_j\rangle. The conclusion is unaffected, but recommend [f]_{ij}=\langle f(u_j),u_i\rangle=\langle u_j,f(u_i)\rangle=\overline{\langle f(u_i),u_j\rangle}=\overline{[f]_{ji}}. The basis vectors u_i of E are also not introduced.
    14. Line 221. “factors into square free linear factors” — recommend “is square free and splits into linear factors over \R”.
    15. Line 225. Over \R the term defined on line 112 is “orthogonally diagonalizable”.
    16. Lines 250–256. The hypothesis should be that u_1,\ldots,u_n is an orthonormal basis. The conclusion “so f is unitary” relies on the later bullet (lines 266–274: an invertible isometry is unitary) and on f being invertible because it maps a basis to a basis. Recommend referring to that bullet or reordering.
    17. Lines 258–264. The display should also note \langle f(v),f(v)\rangle=\langle v,v\rangle before concluding \lambda\overline{\lambda}=1.
    18. Line 273. “so the right side is zero” — recommend “taking v=f^*f(w)-w shows f^*f(w)-w=0”.
    19. Line 293. “L:\image(L)\to\image(L) is the identity map” — recommend “the restriction of L to \image(L) is the identity”.
    20. Line 295. “v=Lx so Lv=Lx=0” is compressed. Recommend “v=Lx=L^2x=Lv=0”.
    21. Line 301. The step \langle L^2u,y\rangle=\langle Lx,Ly\rangle is not justified as written (it holds, but only after further use of self-adjointness and L^2=L). Recommend the direct argument \langle x,y\rangle=\langle Lu,y\rangle=\langle u,Ly\rangle=0.
    22. Line 304. “w,w'=La,La'” — recommend “w=La, w'=La'”.
    23. Line 310. “an orthogonal set of projection maps” — recommend “orthogonal projections p_i onto E(\lambda_i), with p_ip_j=0 for i\ne j and \sum p_i=I”. Also the eigenvalue index runs to k here and to r on line 323; recommend one letter.
    24. Lines 327–328. The paragraph does not state the conclusion. Recommend: “Since L is diagonalizable, f_i(L) acts on E(\lambda_j) as multiplication by f_i(\lambda_j), so f_i(L)=p_i.”
