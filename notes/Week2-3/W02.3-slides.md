## Trace

**Definition.** For an $n\times n$ matrix $A$ over a field $F$, the **trace** is
$$
\trace(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}.
$$

**Proposition.** If $A,B$ are $n\times n$ matrices over $F$, then $\trace(AB)=\trace(BA)$.

## Trace of a Linear Map

Let $f:V\to V$ be linear, $V$ finite dimensional.

**Definition.** Choosing a basis $E$ for $V$, $\trace(f) = \trace([f]_E^E)$.

This is independent of the choice of basis: for a different basis $E'$,
$$
\trace([f]_{E'}^{E'}) = \trace([f]_E^E).
$$

## Trace, Subspaces, and Quotient Spaces

Let $L:V\to V$ be linear and $W\subset V$ an **invariant subspace** ($L(W)\subset W$). Then $L$ induces a well-defined map $\overline{L}:V/W\to V/W$ by $\overline{L}([v])=[L(v)]$.

**Proposition.** With $L_W$ the restriction of $L$ to $W$,
$$
\trace(L) = \trace(L_W) + \trace(\overline{L}).
$$

## Trace and Short Exact Sequences

If $0\to A\to B\to C\to 0$ is a short exact sequence and $L=(L_1,L_2,L_3)$ is a compatible linear map $(A,B,C)\to(A,B,C)$, then
$$
\trace(L_1)-\trace(L_2)+\trace(L_3) = 0.
$$

More generally, for a bounded complex $X^\cdot$ of finite dimensional spaces and a linear map $L:X^\cdot\to X^\cdot$,
$$
\trace(L|X^\cdot) := \sum_i (-1)^i \trace(L_i) = \sum_i (-1)^i \trace(\overline{L}_i),
$$
where $\overline{L}_i$ are the induced maps on cohomology.

## Determinant

**Proposition.** There is a unique function $\det:M_n(F)\to F$ such that:

1. $\det$ is multilinear in the rows of $A$.
2. $\det$ is alternating: it vanishes when two rows are repeated (so swapping two rows negates $\det$).
3. $\det(I)=1$.

## Effect of Row Operations on the Determinant

Assuming $\det$ exists as above:

a. Swapping two rows of $A$: $\det(A)=-\det(A')$.
b. Scaling a row by $a$: $\det(A)=a\det(A')$.
c. Adding a multiple of one row to another: $\det(A)=\det(A')$.

## Existence of the Determinant

- $1\times 1$: $\det(a)=a$. $2\times 2$: $\det\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]=ad-bc$.
- Inductively, with $A_i$ the submatrix deleting row $1$ and column $i$,
$$
\det(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i+1}a_{1i}\det(A_i).
$$

This is the cofactor expansion along the first row (or any row or column).

## Determinant is Multiplicative

**Proposition.** $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$.

**Corollary.** A matrix is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero.

## Permutations and the Determinant

For the standard row vectors $e_i$, $\det(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_n})=\pm 1$; the permutation $i_1,\ldots,i_n$ is **even** if this is $+1$, **odd** if $-1$ — equivalently, according to the parity of the number of exchanges needed to sort it.

**Expansion.**
$$
\det(A) = \sum a_{1i_1}a_{2i_2}\cdots a_{ni_n}\det(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_n}),
$$
a sum of $n!$ terms (the Leibniz formula).

## The Adjugate Matrix

**Definition.** The **adjugate** $A^{adj}$ has entries $b_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\det(A_{ji})$, where $A_{ji}$ deletes row $j$ and column $i$ of $A$.

**Proposition.** $AA^{adj} = A^{adj}A = \det(A)\,I$.

**Remark.** This gives an explicit formula for the inverse: $A^{-1} = \det(A)^{-1}A^{adj}$.

## Cramer's Rule

For $Ax=b$ with $\det(A)\ne 0$,
$$
x_j = \frac{\det(A_j(b))}{\det(A)},
$$
where $A_j(b)$ is $A$ with column $j$ replaced by $b$.

**Remark.** If $A,b$ have integer entries and $\det(A)=\pm 1$, then $x$ has integer entries.

## Determinant of a Linear Map

Let $f:V\to V$ be linear, $V$ finite dimensional over $F$.

**Definition.** $\det(f) = \det([f]_E^E)$ for any basis $E$.

This is well-defined: changing basis conjugates the matrix, which does not change the determinant.

## Determinants and Short Exact Sequences

**Proposition.** If $0\to A_1\to A_2\to A_3\to 0$ is a short exact sequence of finite dimensional spaces and $L=(L_1,L_2,L_3)$ is a compatible linear map, then
$$
\det(L_2) = \det(L_1)\det(L_3).
$$

More generally, for a bounded complex $X^\cdot$ and $L:X^\cdot\to X^\cdot$,
$$
\sum_i (-1)^i \det(L^i:X^i\to X^i) = \sum_i (-1)^i \det(\overline{L}^i : H^i(X^\cdot)\to H^i(X^\cdot)).
$$
