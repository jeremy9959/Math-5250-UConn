## The Matrix Exponential

**Definition.** For a real or complex square matrix $A$, let
$$
\exp(A)=e^A=I+A+\frac{A^2}{2!}+\frac{A^3}{3!}+\cdots
$$

We can make sense of this using the Jordan normal form.

## Reducing to Jordan Form

There is an invertible matrix $P$ such that $B=PAP^{-1}$ is in Jordan form. Since $PA^kP^{-1}=B^k$,
$$
P\exp(A)P^{-1}=I+B+\frac{B^2}{2!}+\frac{B^3}{3!}+\cdots=\exp(B).
$$

So we can define $\exp(A)$ if we can make sense of $\exp(B)$.

The exponential respects the block structure of $B$, so it suffices to compute the powers of a single Jordan block.

## Powers of a Jordan Block

Write a $k\times k$ Jordan block as
$$
J_k(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}
\lambda &         &        &         &         \\
1       & \lambda &        &         &         \\
        & \ddots  & \ddots &         &         \\
        &         & 1      & \lambda &         \\
        &         &        & 1       & \lambda
\end{pmatrix}=\lambda I+N,
\qquad N^k=0.
$$

Since $N$ commutes with $\lambda I$,
$$
(\lambda I+N)^m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}\lambda^{m-j}N^j.
$$

The power $N^j$ is obtained from $N$ by shifting the ones "down and to the left": it has ones on the $j^{th}$ subdiagonal.

## The Exponential of a Jordan Block

It follows that
$$
e^{J_k(\lambda)}=e^{\lambda}\left(I+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{N^j}{j!}\right),
$$
and similarly
$$
e^{tJ_k(\lambda)}=e^{\lambda t}\left(I+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{t^jN^j}{j!}\right).
$$

## First-Order Linear Systems

Consider the system
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\dot{x}_1\\\dot{x}_2\\\vdots\\\dot{x}_n\end{pmatrix}=A\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\\vdots\\x_n\end{pmatrix},
$$
where the $x_i$ are functions of $t$ and $\dot{x}_i=dx_i/dt$.

The solution is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\\vdots\\x_n\end{pmatrix}=\exp(tA)\begin{pmatrix}a_1\\a_2\\\vdots\\a_n\end{pmatrix},
$$
where the $a_i$ are the initial conditions (the values of the $x_i$ at $t=0$).

## Solutions: Diagonal and Jordan Cases

If $A$ is diagonal with entries $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n$, the system breaks up into one-dimensional pieces with solutions
$$
x_i=a_ie^{\lambda_it}.
$$

If $A=J_n(\lambda)$ is a Jordan block, then
$$
\begin{aligned}
x_1&=a_1e^{\lambda t}\\
x_2&=e^{\lambda t}(a_2+ta_1)\\
x_3&=e^{\lambda t}\left(a_3+ta_2+\frac{t^2}{2!}a_1\right)\\
&\;\;\vdots
\end{aligned}
$$
