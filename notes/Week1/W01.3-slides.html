Linear Maps, Change of Basis, Similarity

Math 5250 — Week 1

Coordinates Relative to a Basis

Let L:\R^n\to\R^m be linear, with bases E=\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\} of \R^n and F=\{f_1,\ldots,f_m\} of \R^m.

  • Writing x=\sum x_ie_i, there are unique scalars y_1,\ldots,y_m with L(x)=\sum y_if_i.
  • In coordinates, x=[x]_E and L(x)=[L(x)]_F.
  • L is determined by its values L(e_i) on the basis E.

The Matrix of a Linear Map

Definition. [L]_E^F is the m\times n matrix whose i-th column is L(e_i) expressed in the basis F.

By construction, [L(x)]_F = [L]_E^F\,[x]_E.

Change of Basis

Let E', F' be other bases of \R^n, \R^m, and write e_j=\sum_i a_{ij}e'_i, giving a matrix A=(a_{ij}).

  • A[x]_E = [x]_{E'}, so A=[I]_E^{E'}, the matrix of the identity map from basis E to basis E'.
  • [I]_E^{E'}=\left([I]_{E'}^{E}\right)^{-1}.
  • The matrices of L in the two pairs of bases are related by [L]_E^F = [I]_{F'}^F\,[L]_{E'}^{F'}\,[I]_E^{E'}.

Similarity of Matrices

For L:\R^n\to\R^n and bases E, E', [L]_E^E = P[L]_{E'}^{E'}P^{-1}, \qquad P=[I]_{E'}^E.

Definition. Square matrices A,B are similar if A=PBP^{-1} for some invertible P.

  • All matrices [L]_E^E representing a single linear map L (in different bases) are similar.
  • Proposition. Similar matrices have the same rank and the same nullity. Rank and nullity are therefore well-defined invariants of L, independent of the basis.

Injective, Surjective, and Isomorphisms

  • L:V\to W is injective if and only if its nullity is 0.
  • L:V\to W is surjective if and only if its rank equals \dim W.
  • An isomorphism L:V\to W is a linear map with a linear inverse; equivalently, L is bijective, which forces \dim V=\dim W.

Invertibility Criteria

Proposition. A linear map L:\R^n\to\R^n is invertible if and only if its nullity is 0.

  • An n\times n matrix A is invertible if and only if \mathrm{rank}(A)=n, equivalently \mathrm{nullity}(A)=0.
