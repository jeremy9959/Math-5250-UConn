Vector Spaces, Subspaces, and Dimension

Math 5250 — Week 1

Real and Complex Vectors

An n-dimensional real vector is an n-tuple (x_1,\ldots,x_n) with x_i\in\R; if the x_i are complex, it is an n-dimensional complex vector.

  • Notation: \R^{n}, \C^{n}.
  • Visualize x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\R^n as an arrow from the origin to the point (x_1,\ldots,x_n).
  • Elements of \R or \C are called scalars.

Operations

\R^{n} (or \C^{n}) has two operations:

Addition. If x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n) and y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n) are in \R^n (or \C^n), then x+y=(x_1+y_1,\ldots,x_n+y_n).

Scalar multiplication. If a\in\R (or \C) and y=(y_1,\ldots,y_n), then ay=(ay_1,\ldots,ay_n).

Axioms

These operations satisfy, for x,y\in\R^n and a,b\in\R (likewise for \C):

  1. Addition is commutative and associative; x+e=e+x=x where e=(0,\ldots,0).
  2. Distributivity: (a+b)x=ax+bx and a(x+y)=ax+ay.
  3. Associativity of scalars: a(bx)=(ab)x.
  4. 1x=x and 0x=e.

Vector Spaces

Definition. A vector space over \R is a set V with

  • Addition: x,y\in V \implies x+y\in V;
  • Scalar multiplication: a\in\R,\ x\in V \implies ax\in V,

satisfying the axioms above; in particular V has an element e with x+e=e+x=x for all x.

A vector space over \C is the same, with scalars drawn from \C. \R^n and \C^n are the basic examples.

Subspaces: Definition and Examples

Definition. A subspace W of V is a subset that is itself a vector space under the operations inherited from V.

Example. W=\{(x_1,x_2,0)\}\subset\R^3 is a copy of \R^2 sitting inside \R^3, so it is a subspace.

Example. W=\{x\in\R^n : x_1+\cdots+x_n=0\} is a subspace.

Subspaces: A Non-Example, and a Test

Example. W=\{x\in\R^n : x_1+\cdots+x_n=1\} is not a subspace (it doesn’t contain e).

Proposition (Subspace Test). W\subset V is a subspace provided

  • e\in W,
  • u,v\in W \implies u+v\in W,
  • u\in W,\ a\in\R \implies au\in W.

The same statement holds with \C in place of \R.

Four Crucial Definitions

  • Span of a set of vectors
  • Linear independence
  • Basis

Span of a Set of Vectors

Definition. For S=\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}\subset V, the span of S is either of:

  1. all vectors \sum_{i=1}^k a_iv_i, as (a_1,\ldots,a_k) ranges over \R^k;
  2. the intersection of all subspaces of V containing S.

Linear Independence

Definition. v_1,\ldots,v_n\in V are linearly independent if the only solution of \sum_{i=1}^n a_iv_i=0 is a_1=\cdots=a_n=0.

Linear Independence: Equivalent Characterizations

S=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\} is linearly independent if and only if

  • the span of every proper subset of S is a proper subspace of \mathrm{Span}(S); equivalently,
  • no v_j can be written as a combination of the others: v_j=\sum_{i\ne j}a_iv_i.

Basis

Definition. S\subset V is a basis of V if \mathrm{Span}(S)=V and S is linearly independent.

Dimension

Theorem. Every vector space has a basis.

Theorem. Any two bases X,Y of a vector space V have the same cardinality.

Definition. The dimension of V is the cardinality of any basis of V.

  • V is finite-dimensional if it has a finite basis.
  • Otherwise V is infinite-dimensional.
